# Report — findings and decision framework

Every number and table below is generated by `make report` from `results/runs/*.json` and
`results/quality/*.json`. Nothing here is hand-typed; rebuilding on unchanged results leaves
`git diff` empty. See [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md) for how the measurements are produced
and which of them are not trustworthy.

**What was measured.** 168 latency runs across 6 configurations × 8–10 offered rates × 3
repeats, plus 4 quality evaluations, all on one NVIDIA A40 (sm_86) serving
`meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct` at `max_model_len=4096`. Open-loop Poisson arrivals,
seeded identically across configurations, clocks locked, kernel selection asserted from engine
startup logs.

---

## 1. Headline — the Pareto frontier

![Quality vs cost at a fixed latency budget](results/figures/pareto_quality_cost.png)

Under a p95 TTFT budget of 500 ms, **`vllm-int8-w8a8` is the cost-optimal configuration**: it
serves 2116 output tokens/sec at $0.0525 per million tokens, 27 % cheaper than BF16 while
meeting a tighter tail-latency budget.

**Read the grey band before reading the ranking.** It is BF16's 95 % confidence interval on
GSM8K, and every configuration falls inside it. The quality axis of this frontier is *not
resolved* at this sample size: the spread across all four configurations is 0.0152, smaller
than a single configuration's 95 % half-width of 0.0240. Any claim that INT8 is *more accurate*
than BF16 here would be reading noise. What the chart actually shows is that quality does not
separate these configurations, so the decision collapses onto cost and latency — where the
differences are large and reproducible.

`vllm-bf16` is dominated: three configurations are cheaper at equal-or-better measured quality.
The unquantized baseline is on nobody's frontier.

<!-- BEGIN:cost-note -->
Cost assumes **$0.40/GPU-hour** (RunPod, https://www.runpod.io/pricing, accessed 2026-08-10). The benchmark GPU is a lab machine with no invoice, so this is an assumption. Because every configuration runs on the same GPU, the hourly rate is a linear scalar and the *ranking* is invariant to it — there is no crossover price.

## 2. Finding 1 — decode time tracks weight bytes, not FLOPs

The premise of the whole quantization axis was that A40 decode is memory-bandwidth-bound. If
that is true, shrinking the weights should speed up decode in proportion to the bytes no longer
read. Measured at 1 req/s, where batching is minimal and the bandwidth bound is cleanest:

| Config | Weights | Predicted speedup (byte ratio) | Measured TPOT p95 | Measured speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vllm-bf16` | 15.00 GiB | 1.00× | 32.30 ms | 1.00× |
| `vllm-int8-w8a8` | 8.50 GiB | 1.76× | 18.59 ms | **1.74×** |
| `vllm-gptq-int4` | 5.70 GiB | 2.63× | 10.96 ms | 2.95× |
| `vllm-awq-int4` | 5.70 GiB | 2.63× | 10.33 ms | 3.13× |

**INT8 lands on the prediction within 1.6 %.** That is the cleanest test in the table, because
W8A8 changes weight *and* activation precision together and leaves no dequantization step to
confound the timing. Decode on this GPU is bandwidth-bound, and the bytes-read model predicts
it quantitatively rather than directionally.

**The INT4 configurations beat the prediction, and the bandwidth model does not explain that.**
A pure bytes-read model cannot produce a speedup larger than the byte ratio — any fixed
per-token overhead pushes the measured ratio *below* it, not above. The likely cause is that
the baseline is not itself bandwidth-optimal: Marlin is specifically tuned for the low-batch
regime, while vLLM's general BF16 GEMM path is not, so part of the 2.95–3.13× is kernel quality
rather than byte count. **This benchmark does not isolate that**, and the number is reported as
unexplained rather than claimed as confirmation.

### Act-order overhead, isolated

Both INT4 checkpoints are 4-bit, group size 128, and both select a Marlin kernel. They differ
in that the GPTQ checkpoint carries `desc_act=true`, which `gptq_marlin` handles with a
load-time permutation; AWQ-GEMM has no act-order concept. The pair therefore isolates that
overhead rather than duplicating a data point:

**`gptq_marlin` 10.96 ms vs `awq_marlin` 10.33 ms — a 6.2 % decode penalty for act-order.**
Under saturation the penalty compounds rather than staying constant (§3).

## 3. Finding 2 — the best quantization inverts between load regimes

This is the result that would be missed by any benchmark reporting a single throughput number.

| Offered rate | TPOT p95 winner | TTFT p95 winner |
|---|---|---|
| 1–6 rps | `vllm-awq-int4` (10.3–25.4 ms) | `vllm-int8-w8a8` (96.8–276.0 ms) |
| 7 rps | `vllm-awq-int4` (54.5 ms) | `vllm-int8-w8a8` (330.1 ms) |
| 8 rps | **`vllm-int8-w8a8` (66.7 ms)** | **`vllm-int8-w8a8` (459.0 ms)** |

At 8 req/s the INT4 configurations do not merely lose — they collapse. TTFT p95 goes to
**3288 ms for AWQ and 9594 ms for GPTQ, against 459 ms for INT8**, a 7× and 21× gap against a
configuration that was *behind* on decode latency at every lower rate. BF16 does not reach
8 rps at all; every repeat was classified `oversubscribed`.

**The mechanism is the activation precision, not the weight precision.** Both INT4 formats here
are W4A16: 4-bit weights, 16-bit activations. The GEMM dequantizes weights to FP16 and does
FP16 math, so INT4 buys bandwidth and nothing else. That is exactly right for decode, which is
bandwidth-bound — and worth nothing for prefill, which is compute-bound, where it additionally
pays for the dequantization. W8A8 is different in kind: it feeds the A40's INT8 tensor cores
(~299 TOPS against ~149.7 TFLOPS BF16), so it accelerates prefill *arithmetic*. As offered load
rises, prefill work grows, the bottleneck migrates from bandwidth to compute, and the
configuration that only ever addressed bandwidth runs out of room.

GPTQ collapsing harder than AWQ (9594 vs 3288 ms) is the act-order penalty compounding: a
6.2 % per-token cost at low load becomes a queueing catastrophe once the server is at capacity,
because the service rate falls below the arrival rate and the queue grows without bound.

**Practical consequence.** "Which quantization is fastest" has no load-independent answer on
this hardware. Below the knee, pick for decode bandwidth: INT4. At or near the knee, pick for
prefill compute: INT8. A benchmark that reports one rate cannot see this.

## 4. Decision framework — cost-optimal configuration vs SLA

Given a p95 TTFT budget, the cheapest configuration that meets it:

<!-- BEGIN:sla-table -->
| p95 TTFT budget | Cheapest config | Rate | Throughput | $/1M tokens | Admissible |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 ms | `vllm-awq-int4` | 4 rps | 1112 tok/s | $0.0999 | 6 of 6 |
| 500 ms | `vllm-int8-w8a8` | 8 rps | 2116 tok/s | $0.0525 | 6 of 6 |
| 1000 ms | `vllm-int8-w8a8` | 8 rps | 2116 tok/s | $0.0525 | 6 of 6 |
| 2000 ms | `vllm-int8-w8a8` | 8 rps | 2116 tok/s | $0.0525 | 6 of 6 |
<!-- END:sla-table -->

The crossover at the tight end is real: at a 200 ms budget no configuration sustains its
highest rate, so the ranking is decided by which one still meets the budget at 4 rps, and
`vllm-awq-int4` wins on cost there. From 500 ms upward `vllm-int8-w8a8` wins, and the answer
stops changing — not because the budget stops mattering, but because the measured ladder tops
out at 8 rps. **Above roughly 500 ms the binding constraint is engine capacity, not the SLA**,
and a further-loosened budget buys nothing. Extending the ladder past 8 rps for INT8 would be
the natural next measurement; every rate above it in the current matrix is `oversubscribed`
and therefore unreportable.

### How this transfers off the A40

The mechanism generalizes even though the numbers do not. The question to ask of any
accelerator is where its arithmetic intensity sits relative to the model's: the A40 is
compute-rich and bandwidth-poor (~215 FLOP/byte against ~153 for an A100-80GB), which is why
weight-only quantization pays here more than published A100 results suggest, and why the
prefill regime flips to favouring a format with real low-precision compute. On a part with FP8
tensor cores (sm_89/sm_90) the W8A8 role would be filled by FP8 rather than INT8 — which is
precisely why FP8 was excluded here rather than faked (§7).

### Quality

Greedy decoding, fixed seed, identical prompts, measured **through the serving engine** so the
quantized kernels are the ones actually exercised. GSM8K is 8-shot on the completions endpoint;
IFEval is 0-shot with the chat template applied.

<!-- BEGIN:quality-table -->
| Config | Weights | WikiText-2 PPL | ΔPPL | GSM8K | Δ | IFEval | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vllm-bf16` | 15.0 GiB | 6.3545 | +0.0000 | 0.7293 | +0.0000 | 0.7449 | +0.0000 |
| `vllm-int8-w8a8` | 8.5 GiB | 6.4400 | +0.0855 | 0.7445 | +0.0152 | 0.7579 | +0.0129 |
| `vllm-awq-int4` | 5.7 GiB | 6.6619 | +0.3074 | 0.7316 | +0.0023 | 0.7449 | +0.0000 |
| `vllm-gptq-int4` | 5.7 GiB | 6.8642 | +0.5098 | 0.7407 | +0.0114 | 0.7375 | -0.0074 |
<!-- END:quality-table -->

**Perplexity separates these configurations; the task benchmarks do not.** WikiText-2 perplexity
orders them monotonically by bit width, with a spread well outside measurement noise. GSM8K and
IFEval place all four within each other's confidence intervals. This is not a defect of the
tasks — it is a statement about statistical power: at ~1300 GSM8K samples the resolvable
difference is a few points, and quantization damage here is smaller than that. Reporting the
task scores as a quality *ranking* would be overclaiming; they are reported as evidence that no
configuration is grossly broken, which is what they can actually support.

## 6. The engine axis

SGLang was run as a reduced confirmation sweep, not as a co-equal subject — quantization is the
primary axis and got the GPU-hours. Two observations:

**Below saturation the engines are indistinguishable.** At 1 rps, BF16 delivers 265 tok/s on
both, with TTFT p95 141.9 ms (vLLM) against 143.5 ms (SGLang). At 4 rps, 1028 against
1026 tok/s. Whatever the quantization findings above are, they are not vLLM artifacts.

**At saturation SGLang degrades more gracefully.** At 8 rps, `sglang-bf16` remains reportable
at TTFT p95 2670 ms where `vllm-bf16` is oversubscribed in every repeat, and `sglang-awq-int4`
holds 1465 ms against vLLM's 3288 ms for the same checkpoint and kernel. This is a scheduling
and admission difference, not a kernel difference — both engines select `awq_marlin`, asserted
from their startup logs. It was not investigated further and is reported as an observation.

**A confound applies to this section specifically.** Both SGLang configurations were measured
with a busy neighbour GPU in the same chassis, while most of the vLLM ladder was not. That
stamp travels with every affected record. Its measured effect across the 123 valid runs is
1.1 °C *cooler* at 7 MHz *higher* clocks for busy-neighbour runs, with zero throttled samples
anywhere — the opposite direction to the feared effect, and small. The comparison is reported
with the asterisk rather than without it.

## 7. Limitations

- **One model, one GPU, one context length.** Llama-3.1-8B at `max_model_len=4096` on a single
  A40. Nothing here establishes behaviour at 70B, at long context, or across tensor parallelism.
- **The INT4 decode speedup exceeds what the bandwidth model predicts** and this benchmark does
  not isolate why (§2). The INT8 result, which does fit, is the load-bearing evidence.
- **The quality axis is unresolved.** All four configurations lie inside one another's
  confidence intervals on both task benchmarks (§5). Perplexity separates them; task accuracy
  does not, and the Pareto frontier's quality ordering should not be read as significant.
- **SGLang has no quality measurement.** Quality was run for the four vLLM configurations only,
  so SGLang appears on the latency and cost axes but not on the frontier.
- **FP8 is absent by design, not by omission.** The A40 is sm_86 and has no FP8 tensor cores.
  vLLM will still *load* an FP8 checkpoint on Ampere by dequantizing to FP16 — it appears to
  work, yields weight-only compression, and delivers no compute speedup. Publishing that as
  "FP8 on A40" is the error class this repository exists to avoid.
- **`ignore_eos=True`** forces every configuration to perform identical work, which is what
  makes the comparison fair and simultaneously makes the output lengths less realistic than the
  input lengths. The tradeoff is deliberate and stated in METHODOLOGY.md §2.
- **The rate ladder tops out below INT8's capacity** (§4). INT8's true saturation point was not
  found; it is above 8 rps.
- **The cost figure is a public reference price, not an invoice.** The measurement GPU is a lab
  machine. Because every configuration runs on the same GPU, the price is a linear scalar and
  cannot reorder the ranking — which is why no price-sensitivity table appears here.
